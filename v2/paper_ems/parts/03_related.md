# 2. Related work

## 2.1 Integrating agricultural statistics with environmental data

Agricultural systems science depends on data infrastructures that link observations, models and decision tools [@janssen2017]. Several recent datasets harmonise sub-national crop statistics: for the European Union with quality flags for each record [@ronchetti2024], and for 33 countries of sub-Saharan Africa [@lee2025]. Global gridded yield products combine national statistics with satellite data [@iizumi2020]. Crop yield forecasting combines statistics with weather and remote-sensing predictors in modular workflows [@paudel2021] and in benchmark datasets that pair sub-national yields with weather, remote-sensing and soil predictors [@kallenberg2026]; at field scale, multi-source pipelines automate the assembly of environmental predictors for yield models [@khidirova2026]. On the satellite side, Earth-observation data cubes provide analysis-ready imagery [@lewis2017; @giuliani2017], openEO harmonises access to cloud processing back-ends [@schramm2021], and Google Earth Engine provides planetary-scale analysis of public archives [@gorelick2017].

In these systems official statistics generally enter as already tabulated inputs, and the work of digitising them, and of verifying the digitised values, is outside their scope. In Bangladesh, BBS district statistics have been used for national time-series and district trend analyses [@shahrier2025; @almamun2024], but without a published, checkable extraction. Table 1 summarises the datasets and frameworks closest to this study.

The two closest frameworks show the difference in emphasis. UniCrop automates the acquisition, cleaning and harmonisation of satellite, meteorological, soil and terrain predictors for a given location, crop and period, and is evaluated by the accuracy of yield models trained on 557 field observations of rice [@khidirova2026]: its target data are field observations, and its evaluation concerns prediction rather than the integration steps. CY-Bench pairs sub-national yield statistics, mostly from national statistics offices, with weather, remote-sensing and soil predictors aggregated to administrative units and aligned to the crop season; its yield data are filtered for zero or missing values and checked for consistency between yield, production and area, and their sources are documented in country-level data cards [@kallenberg2026]. As far as we can determine from these publications, neither digitises statistics from PDF publications, checks the statistics against the publisher's printed totals or a second edition, records the provenance of each value, or reports tests in which known errors are introduced to show that its checks detect them. These are the elements this study adds.

## 2.2 Harmonising spatial units and names

Linking statistics across years and sources requires a stable set of spatial units. Boundary changes complicate longitudinal census data [@gregory2005] and have motivated longitudinal databases that re-express historical data in present-day units [@logan2014]; global boundary datasets document the version and source of each unit [@runfola2020; @gadm2022]. Even where boundaries are stable, unit names differ between sources through transliteration, abbreviations and official spelling changes. Matching such names is a record-linkage problem [@christen2012]. Fuzzy string matching resolves many variants automatically, but it can also assign a name to the wrong unit without warning, and the rules applied are seldom published with the dataset. The approach taken here is the opposite: an explicit list of observed variants, published with match statistics, and a pipeline that stops on any name it does not recognise.

## 2.3 Reproducible research software

Recommendations for reproducible computational research include scripting every step, recording software versions and sharing code and data [@peng2011; @sandve2013; @wilson2017; @stodden2016], and the FAIR principles have been extended from data to research software [@wilkinson2016; @barker2022]. Practice lags behind: few computational hydrology studies can be reproduced from their publications [@hutton2016], and assessments of geoscientific and GIScience papers found that many could not be reproduced, most often because data, code or details of the computational environment were missing [@konkol2019; @nust2018]. A taxonomy of reproducible and replicable research has been proposed for environmental modelling [@essawy2020]. Reproducibility claims are, however, usually supported by the availability of code and data rather than by a documented rebuild in a new environment with a stated criterion for what counts as the same result.

## 2.4 Data engineering architectures and data validation

Lakehouse platforms combine open file formats with warehouse-style management of analytical tables [@armbrust2021]; the Bronze–Silver–Gold ("medallion") layering commonly used with them separates data as received from cleaned and integrated data, and embedded analytical databases such as DuckDB make the pattern practical on a single computer [@raasveldt2019]. Workflow managers such as Snakemake and targets rebuild only the steps whose inputs have changed and record the dependencies between them [@molder2021; @landau2021]. Data validation has been formalised as unit tests for data, declarative constraints checked on every batch [@schelter2018], and validating data is a central challenge in production machine-learning pipelines [@polyzotis2018]. Data quality has several dimensions beyond accuracy, including completeness, consistency and interpretability [@wang1996], and provenance records how each value was derived [@simmhan2005]. Open frameworks for environmental data handle climate data [@iturbide2019] and agricultural land-use data on Google Earth Engine [@zhang2020], both in this journal, and Earth-observation processing in agroecology [@graf2022].

Testing a system by deliberately introducing faults is established in software engineering and has been applied to the input data of machine-learning systems, one fault at a time [@rahal2023]. Generic constraint checks (types, ranges, uniqueness, completeness) are necessary but cannot detect a misread or shifted cell whose value is still plausible. Official statistics, however, contain internal redundancy that can be checked: the same quantity in two units, district values that must sum to a printed total, and the same crop year printed in successive editions. We are not aware of an open framework that combines such value-level checks of digitised official statistics with explicit name harmonisation, crop-calendar alignment and satellite covariates, and that reports evidence that its checks detect deliberately introduced errors and that its outputs can be rebuilt in a clean environment.
